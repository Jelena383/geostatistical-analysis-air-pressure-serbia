# Geostatistical Analysis of Air Pressure Distribution in Serbia Using GIS and Spatial Statistics

This repository presents a comparative GIS based geostatistical analysis of air pressure distribution across Serbia using multiple interpolation methods and hotspot spatial statistics.

The project integrates Geographic Information Systems (GIS), geostatistics, spatial interpolation, hotspot analysis, and comparative spatial modeling in order to evaluate spatial patterns of air pressure distribution and analyze differences between interpolation approaches.

The workflow demonstrates how GIS based geostatistical methods can support spatial prediction, environmental analysis, climatological interpretation, and spatial statistical modeling.

# Project Objective

The primary objective of the study was to compare multiple geostatistical interpolation methods and evaluate their ability to model spatial air pressure distribution across Serbia and neighboring regions.

The analysis aimed to:

- visualize spatial distribution of air pressure values,
- compare interpolation methodologies,
- evaluate spatial prediction behavior between models,
- identify spatial clustering patterns,
- and demonstrate the application of GIS based spatial statistics in climatological analysis.

# Study Area

The analysis covered the territory of Serbia together with surrounding neighboring regions in order to ensure spatial continuity and improve interpolation reliability near national borders.

The study included meteorological stations located within:

- Serbia,
- Hungary,
- Croatia,
- Bosnia and Herzegovina,
- Montenegro,
- Albania,
- North Macedonia,
- Bulgaria,
- and Romania.

The inclusion of neighboring territories enabled more realistic interpolation of atmospheric spatial patterns.

# Data and Software

The analysis was based on data collected from:

- 60 meteorological stations distributed across Serbia and neighboring territories.

Five stations containing invalid air pressure values were excluded from the analysis in order to improve interpolation reliability.

The study was conducted using:

- ArcGIS Pro 3.5,
- GeoDa,
- GIS spatial analysis tools,
- and geostatistical interpolation workflows.

# GIS and Geostatistical Methodology

The workflow integrated several interpolation and spatial statistical approaches in order to compare different geostatistical modeling techniques.

The following methods were applied:

- Kriging interpolation,
- Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW),
- Spline interpolation,
- Natural Neighbor interpolation,
- and Hot Spot analysis.

# Kriging Interpolation

Kriging represents an advanced geostatistical interpolation method that predicts values at unsampled locations using spatial dependence between measured points.

The method incorporates spatial autocorrelation and semivariogram based modeling in order to generate statistically optimized prediction surfaces.

Kriging was performed using:

- ArcGIS Pro Geostatistical Wizard.

# Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW)

Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW) is a deterministic interpolation method based on the assumption that nearby observations exert stronger influence on predicted values than distant observations.

The method emphasizes local spatial relationships and generates continuous spatial prediction surfaces.

# Spline Interpolation

Spline interpolation uses mathematical polynomial functions in order to generate smooth continuous surfaces between known data points.

The method is particularly useful for representing gradual spatial transitions and continuous environmental variables.

# Natural Neighbor Interpolation

Natural Neighbor interpolation estimates values using weighted influence from surrounding neighboring points while preserving local spatial characteristics.

The method is effective for irregularly distributed spatial datasets and minimizes unrealistic surface artifacts.

# Hot Spot Analysis

Spatial statistical hotspot analysis was conducted using:

- GeoDa Local G* analysis,
- and ArcGIS Pro Getis-Ord Gi* analysis.

Unlike interpolation methods, Hot Spot analysis does not generate continuous prediction surfaces.

Instead, it statistically evaluates whether high or low values form significant spatial clusters.

The workflow enabled identification of:

- Hot Spots,
- Cold Spots,
- and Non-significant spatial clusters.

# Comparative Spatial Analysis

The project compared interpolation methods according to:

- spatial prediction behavior,
- smoothness of generated surfaces,
- representation of local spatial variability,
- and visualization of atmospheric spatial patterns.

The comparative approach enabled better understanding of methodological differences between deterministic and geostatistical interpolation techniques.
